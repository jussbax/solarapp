# Round 5 — engineer's review of the thirteen drone frames

All thirteen originals read at full size, arrays re-read at 2.5–3× (crops in this folder). Counts are by frame lines: the thin line across the middle of every module is the half-cut split, not a panel edge, which is where "about twenty" and "six, ten and four" came from. No original carries EXIF, so the hour is read from shadows. Setbacks are scaled from the module's short side (about 1.1 m, verify from the datasheet) and are rough. No wattage or kWp is stated or implied.

## 1. What the frames show
**Site A, 25–28 (rib-type roof, one face).** Eight portrait modules in two rows of four, each row on two rails across the slope on the purlin lines; end and mid clamps visible (26 at 1.6×) at roughly 15–20 % and 73–75 % of the module length, not symmetric: verify against the module's clamping zone. Fastener heads, sealing and purlin engagement cannot be seen in any frame. Setbacks: ridge and eave ~0.3 m; rake ~0.6 m for the upper row but ~0.2–0.3 m for the lower row, which is shifted toward the rake by a third to half a module (all four frames): laid where the fixings landed. Flush at the roof's tilt and azimuth. An orange flexible run (25 at 3×) leaves from under the lower-left module at the junction-box position and loops unclipped over the rake flashing, no drip loop. No bonding jumper, label or conduit visible: verify. Site clean.

**Site B, 29–34 and 36 (grey corrugated hip roof, built-up street).** Three faces, not four: left triangular face, one column of six landscape modules on two rails down the slope (rail ends protrude, 31 at 2×); top face, six portrait along the eave plus two portrait below on their own rails; front face, two portrait under the ridge; right face empty in every frame. Total 16. Top row ~0.1–0.2 m from the eave, left column ~0.2 m from its eave (31, 36). Cables (33, 34, 36 at 1.6×): three black runs lie unsupported in the corrugation valleys, over the hip to the top-face pair, over the ridge cap to the front pair, and across open roof to the left eave and off it; no conduit, clips or drip loop. That is a job before the cable work: a competitor says temporary, a PEE writes a punch-list item. Install day (29–32): hose, bin, carton, coiled cord, bags, gear in the courtyard, four people at the gate (31, 32; no consent). In 36 a cable coil, a box and gear still sit on the lower roof at bottom-left, so 36 is not "after switch-on". A dark spot by the green patch beside the top-face pair (31): old hole or fastener, verify sealed. The neighbour's green roof carries four modules (31, 32, a sliver at 36's left edge); neighbours' balconies and laundry cross the top of 29, 30, 33, 34, 36.

**Site C, 35 and 37 (three roofs of one property).** Main house 2 rows × 9 portrait = 18; annex one row of 9 portrait = 9; small roof 2 rows × 5 portrait = 10; total 37. Main array well in from both rakes but offset toward the palms (~0.9 m palm side, ~1.6 m other, verify); small-roof rows run to ~0.15–0.3 m of the edges. Shade of two kinds: palm fronds across four or five modules of the main array's upper row (35) / the column nearer the palms (37); and, worse and absent from the caption, a continuous band over the top third of all nine annex modules cast by the main house's eave (35 at 2.5×; along the right side in 37). A band across one cell group of every module in a string trips every bypass diode: about a third of the string's power gone while it lasts (verify against the string layout). Hour: the house's own shadow (35) and palm shadows several crown-widths long (37) mean a low sun, morning or late afternoon; which depends on the roof's orientation, which the frames do not give: verify from the roof-visit record. Publishing invites "why under a tree?" and, from a PEE, "why under the eave?". Also in frame: a striped tarp (35, 37), rubbish at the neighbour's wall (35 bottom-left). No cable or conduit visible on any roof: verify.
