How net metering works, in four lines.

  1. By day The panels power the house first. Whatever is left goes to the grid through a two-way meter.
  2. At night The house draws from the grid as before.
  3. On the bill Your electric company credits the power you sent at its generation rate (the rate it pays for power, lower than the rate you pay). That is why the bill shrinks but does not reach zero.
  4. The papers We file the net metering application, the ERC certificate of compliance (the net metering certificate) and the meter request. You sign two forms. Between switch-on and the two-way meter the panels already cut your daytime bill.

No battery means no backup.

We say it before you buy: a net-metered system without a battery switches off during a brownout, as the safety rules require, and restarts on its own when the grid returns. If brownouts are what keep you up at night, read about solar with a battery; the estimate shows that price too, and a battery can be added later.
